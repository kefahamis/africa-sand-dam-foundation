Water Access

Providing clean, reliable water to communities across Kenya's arid lands

Overview

Water Access is the cornerstone of everything ASDF does. In Kenya's semi-arid regions, communities spend hours every day walking to collect water — often of poor quality. Our sand dam technology changes that permanently.

What We Do

We work alongside communities to construct sand dams across seasonal riverbeds. As floods pass, the dams trap sand which stores clean sub-surface water available year-round, even in prolonged droughts.

  • Sand dam construction with community-led labour
  • Shallow well drilling adjacent to sand dams
  • Solar-powered pipeline systems
  • Rock catchment infrastructure
  • School rainwater harvesting tanks

Our Focus Areas

Our water access work combines several complementary technologies, each suited to a different part of the landscape:

Rock Catchments

On exposed granite outcrops we channel seasonal rainfall off the rock surface into lined storage tanks. Rock catchments capture large volumes of clean water quickly and serve communities where sub-surface storage isn't viable.

Sand Dams

Our flagship technology: reinforced concrete walls built across seasonal riverbeds that trap sand as floodwaters pass. The sand stores clean sub-surface water that remains available year-round, even through prolonged droughts.

School Water Tanks

We install rainwater harvesting tanks at schools so children have safe drinking water on site. Reliable water keeps pupils — especially girls — healthy and in class instead of walking long distances to fetch water.

Shallow Wells

Hand-dug shallow wells sited next to sand dams give households an easy, protected point to draw the stored water for drinking, cooking and small-scale irrigation close to home.

Solar Pipelines

Solar-powered pumps and pipeline networks move water from dams and wells to kiosks, tanks and farms further afield — extending clean water access well beyond the riverbed at no ongoing fuel cost.

Our Impact

Since 2010, ASDF has constructed over 734 sand dams serving more than 1.1 million community members. Each dam costs a fraction of conventional water infrastructure and lasts generations with minimal maintenance.
